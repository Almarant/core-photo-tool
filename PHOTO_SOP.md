# Core tray photography — field protocol

One page. Most of the quality of the final image set is decided here, not in the
processing. Every rule below exists because breaking it cost us something on the
DD26ZOP-006 / -007 batch.

## Framing

**Get the whole tray in the frame, with a hand's width of floor on every side.**
In the 2026 batch the tray ran past the frame edge in most photos. Nothing —
manual or automatic — recovers core that was outside the frame. Those images now
carry grey padding at the ends.

Shoot straight down on the tray centre. Small tilt is corrected automatically, so
don't agonise over it, but don't shoot from the end of the tray.

**Keep the camera height fixed for a whole hole.** Mark the tripod legs on the
floor. The processing measures scale per photo, but a constant height makes
everything more reliable and lets trays be compared directly.

## The label bar

Every photo must show the bar with **hole ID, tray number, and END DEPTH**. This
is the only record that ties the image to the hole, and it is what the naming
step reads.

- Lay the bar in the same place each time — just above the tray's top rail.
- Keep the letter tiles clean and complete. A missing digit means someone has to
  chase the depth in the logs later.
- Depths in metres, two decimals, same format every time (`86.00`, not `86` or
  `86,0`).

## The tape measure

**The tape must be in every frame.** It is not just a scale — the processing uses
its white surface as the colour reference to correct exposure and white balance.
No tape in frame means no colour correction for that photo.

Lay it flat across the tray, fully in frame end to end.

## Dry and wet

Shoot **dry first, then wet**, and shoot both before moving the tray.

The processing decides which is which by brightness (wet core is darker), not by
order, so an occasional reversal is handled. But keeping the order consistent
makes review much easier.

Wet the core evenly. A half-wet tray is worse than a dry one — it reads as
neither.

## Lighting

Diffuse, even light. Avoid:

- **Direct sun** across part of the tray. Shade the whole tray or none of it.
- **Your own shadow** falling across the tray — watch where you stand.
- **Mixed light sources** (shed lights plus daylight through a door). Close the
  door or turn the lights off; pick one.

Wet core is glossy, so a single hard light source produces a bright specular
streak down each run. Diffuse light avoids it.

## Keep the floor clear

Boots, hoses, buckets and cable in the frame next to the tray confuse automatic
tray detection. Take two seconds to kick them out of shot.

## Camera settings

- Fixed white balance (daylight or a custom preset) — not auto. Auto white
  balance drifts shot to shot and fights the correction step.
- Fixed exposure for a session if the camera allows it.
- Highest quality JPEG, or RAW if you have the storage.
- **Do not** apply in-camera filters, "vivid" modes, or beauty/HDR processing.

## Files

Copy the card to a folder per hole, named for the hole. Don't rename anything by
hand — the processing does it, and hand-renaming breaks the pairing.

Back up the originals before processing. The processing never modifies them, but
that is no substitute for a copy.

## If a colour checker card is available

Put one in the frame, at the tray end, in every photo. It is strictly better than
the tape as a colour reference and would let the correction handle colour casts
properly rather than just neutralising them. Cheap, and worth it if this photo
set will be used for any quantitative colour or mineralogy work later.
